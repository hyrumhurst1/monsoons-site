Mesa Company Monsoons AI Gets East Valley Businesses Named by Google AI Overviews, Gemini and ChatGPT

New Arizona service measures whether the AI answers on Google, Gemini and ChatGPT recommend a local business, and does not charge the balance until they do.

MESA, Ariz. — When someone in Mesa searches for a plumber, a dentist or somewhere to eat, the answer increasingly arrives as a short list of business names written by AI and placed at the top of the page, before the familiar results. Monsoons AI, a new company based in Mesa, works to get local businesses onto that list, and measures whether it happened.

The work is known in the industry as answer engine optimization, or AEO, and sometimes as generative engine optimization, or GEO. Both describe the same shift: being named inside an AI-written answer rather than ranked in a list of links. Google’s AI answers, Gemini, ChatGPT and Perplexity all now respond to a question like “who should I hire to fix my water heater in Mesa” with a handful of recommendations. A business absent from that answer has no equivalent of a second page to appear on.

The commercial argument is about position. On Google, the AI answer commonly renders above the organic results — the part of the page most people see first — and unlike the ad slots on that same page, there is no way to buy a place inside it. A business earns the mention or it does not appear.

“Being fourth on Google was survivable. Being left out of a three-name answer is not. And you cannot outspend your way into it. That is the part local owners find hardest to believe and easiest to verify — it takes about ten minutes to see what AI currently says about your business.”
— Hyrum Hurst, founder, Monsoons AI

Monsoons AI serves Mesa, Gilbert, Chandler, Tempe, Queen Creek and Apache Junction. Its focus is storefront and service businesses: restaurants, cafes, salons, dentists, auto repair shops, gyms, and home-service trades including plumbing, HVAC, landscaping and junk removal.

The method

A baseline first, then the same questions again

The company’s work has two parts. It publishes factual written coverage of a business across independent websites and makes the business’s details consistent everywhere an answer engine checks, including its Google Business Profile. It then measures the result against a baseline recorded before any work began.

Monsoons agrees a fixed set of questions with the client, records how the assistants answer them before anything is published, then re-runs the identical set afterward and compares. Each question is run repeatedly rather than once, because answer engines do not return the same answer twice, and a single screenshot proves nothing.

Every run is made from a clean session with no account history. A business owner’s own logged-in ChatGPT is personalised to them and will show results a prospective customer never sees — which is why owners are often surprised in both directions when they check properly.

What the company does

Two problems, and a website if the site is the problem

Getting named. The core service is getting a business into the short list an assistant gives when a nearby customer asks who to hire.

Correcting what assistants already say. The second problem is less obvious and, for some businesses, more urgent. Assistants answer confidently whether or not their information is right. They give closed hours, services a business stopped offering, an old phone number, or details belonging to a similarly named competitor. Monsoons audits what the assistants currently say and corrects the underlying sources, so the answer matches the business.

“Most owners assume that if AI is wrong about them there is nothing to be done. There usually is. The answer is coming from sources you can actually fix.”
— Hyrum Hurst

Websites, where the site is what is holding them back. The company builds sites using AI tooling, which keeps the cost down and often replaces a monthly subscription a business is paying for a page it does not control. Clients own the result outright and can have Monsoons maintain it or not.

How it is bought

Pick what you want. One job, or ongoing.

Monsoons is deliberately modular. A business can buy a single piece of work and be done, or have the company build something and keep it maintained. There is no bundle a client has to take to get the part they came for.

Ongoing work is a monthly retainer that can be cancelled any month. One-off work is quoted and finished. The company publishes what each package includes and what it costs to start, rather than requiring a call to hear a number.

The guarantee

The balance is owed only when the standard is met

The measurement standard is set in writing before work starts. The questions, the assistants, the share of them that has to show the business cited or recommended, and the deadline are all agreed and signed before anything is published — so there is no argument afterward about what counted.

A miss means a second campaign at the company’s cost. A second miss means the balance is refunded. The exact thresholds are published in full at monsoonsai.com.

What is not promised

Three things the company says it cannot do

Monsoons AI publishes what it will not promise alongside what it will. The company states that it cannot promise a first-place position, because answer engines do not return ranked lists; that visibility gained from any single campaign fades as competitors publish; and that it does not guarantee a number of leads or a revenue figure.

Common questions

What business owners ask about AI search

Why isn’t my business showing up in ChatGPT?

Usually because the information an answer engine can find about you is thin or inconsistent. Engines build a picture of a business from many independent sources. If those sources disagree on your name, phone or services, or barely exist, you get left out of the answer.

How do I get my business recommended by ChatGPT?

Make the same facts about your business true in enough independent places that the engine treats them as settled: a complete Google Business Profile, consistent details everywhere they appear, published coverage on sites the engine already reads, and recent reviews.

What is answer engine optimization?

Work aimed at getting a business named inside an AI assistant’s answer, rather than ranked in a list of links. Assistants return a few names and no page two, so the goal shifts from placement to inclusion.

Is this different from SEO?

Yes. SEO is about where you land in a list of links. This is about whether you get named in an answer that has no links in it. Different mechanism, different work, and it moves faster. They do not conflict.

Does my restaurant or shop actually need to worry about this?

It depends whether your customers ask AI assistants for recommendations yet. The way to find out is to run the questions your customers would ask and see who gets named. Monsoons does that free.
